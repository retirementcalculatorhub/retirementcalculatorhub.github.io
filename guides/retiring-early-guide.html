Retiring Early: A Guide

What early retirement really costs, how to plan for a longer retirement, and the trade-offs to think about.

Retiring early is an attractive idea, and for some people it is achievable, but it is expensive in two ways: you save for fewer years, and your savings must last for more years. Both effects push the target up sharply. This guide explains the maths, the trade-offs, and the practical steps for anyone seriously considering it.

The double cost of retiring early

Retiring at 55 instead of 65 costs you ten years of contributions and ten years of growth, and it adds ten years of spending that the savings must fund. A 40-year-old saving 1,000 a month at 7 percent reaches about 810,000 by 65, but only about 380,000 by 55. The gap is not just the missing contributions; it is the missing compounding, which is largest in the final years. Early retirement therefore needs either a much higher saving rate, a lower spending level, or income in retirement from part-time work.

How much more you need

A common way to think about it is the 25-times rule, applied to a longer retirement. Retiring at 55 with a 40-year retirement needs a larger multiple than retiring at 65 with a 30-year one, often 30 times annual spending or more, because the money must last longer and the withdrawal rate must be lower. If you expect to spend 40,000 a year, the target at 65 is about 1,000,000, but at 55 it is closer to 1,200,000, and you have ten fewer years to save it. The exact figure depends on returns and spending, and the calculator can test your own numbers.

Worked example

A 30-year-old earning 70,000 a year wants to retire at 55, spending 40,000 a year. The target is about 1,200,000. They have 10,000 saved. Saving 1,500 a month at 7 percent gives about 1,220,000 by 55, so the plan works, but it needs a saving rate of about 26 percent of income, before any employer match. Saving 1,000 a month gives only about 815,000, which is not enough. The example shows that early retirement is possible, but it needs a high saving rate sustained for decades.

Income in early retirement

Many early retirees do not stop working entirely; they switch to part-time, freelance, or a lower-paid job they enjoy. Even a modest income of 10,000 a year in early retirement reduces the amount savings must provide, and it can make the difference between a plan that works and one that does not. It also provides structure and social contact, which many retirees miss more than they expect. Plan for the possibility that you will want some work, rather than assuming you will never earn again.

The non-financial side

Retirement is not only about money. People who retire early sometimes find the loss of structure, colleagues and purpose harder than they expected. Before committing to an early date, test the lifestyle: take a long holiday, try the hobbies you plan to fill your time with, and talk to people who retired early about what surprised them. Health matters too: retiring early gives you more healthy years to enjoy, which is a real benefit, but it also means a longer period without employer health cover, which can be costly. A good early retirement plan covers health, purpose and money, not just money.

Early retirement and part-time work, combined

Take a 40-year-old who wants to stop full-time work at 55 but is willing to work part-time until 62, earning 15,000 dollars a year. The part-time income covers 15,000 of annual spending for seven years, or 105,000 in total, and it also means seven fewer years of full withdrawals from savings. The effect on the target is large: instead of needing 1,200,000 at 55, they need about 950,000, because the part-time years reduce the burden on the pot. The monthly saving needed falls from about 1,500 to about 1,100, which is far more achievable. The example shows that early retirement does not have to mean no work at all, and that a few years of part-time income can make an impossible plan possible.

Tip: Retiring at 55 instead of 65 costs ten years of contributions and growth, and adds ten years of spending — the target rises from about 1,000,000 to about 1,200,000 for 40,000 a year of spending.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do I need to retire early?

More than for retiring at 65, because the money must last longer. A common figure is about 30 times annual spending for retiring at 55, versus 25 times at 65.

Can I retire early on a normal salary?

It is possible with a high saving rate sustained for decades, or with part-time income in retirement. Use the calculator to test your own numbers.

What is the biggest cost of retiring early?

The missing decade of compounding. The final years of saving add the most growth, and retiring early gives them up.

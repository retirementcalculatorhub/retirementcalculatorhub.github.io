How It Works

Our retirement calculator projects your savings to retirement using compound growth: your current savings grow, your monthly contributions grow, and the result is shown both in future dollars and in today's money after inflation. Try it in the main calculator, then use the monthly contribution calculator to find the saving rate that reaches your target.

If you want the method, how to use the retirement calculator explains each input, compound interest and retirement shows why starting early matters, and how much to save for retirement explains the 25-times rule with a worked example.

For the later stages, the nest egg drawdown planner shows how much you can withdraw each month, and making savings last explains the 4 percent guideline. Do not forget inflation and retirement — at 3 percent, prices double in about 24 years.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do I need to retire?

A common rule is 25 times your annual retirement spending. If you expect to spend 40,000 a year, the target is about 1,000,000.

How much should I save each month?

It depends on your target, current savings, years left and return. A 35-year-old with 20,000 saved needs about 690 a month to reach 1,000,000 by 65 at 7 percent.

Is it too late to start at 40?

No. You still have 20 to 25 years, and income is often at its highest. The monthly amount matters more now than starting early did.

What return should I assume?

Around 7 percent a year before inflation is a common planning figure, but real returns vary. Test 5 percent too.

What is the 4 percent rule?

Withdraw 4 percent of your starting balance in the first year, then adjust for inflation. It has historically lasted about 30 years, but it is a guideline, not a guarantee.

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. The calculation runs in your browser, and nothing you enter is sent or stored.
